I am the inaugural social scientist at [The Peregrine Fund](https://peregrinefund.org), a global conservation organization focused on birds of prey. I work on why people interact with nature (especially birds) the way they do, and what drives meaningful and sustainable changes in behavior, rather than just attitudes. Conservation is a particularly interesting collective behavior problem: How should society allocate resources to maintain a complex system, based on limited evidence and conflicting incentives? I apply methods developed outside of conservation, in development economics, public policy, social psychology, and complexity science.

I earned my B.A. in Mathematics with a minor in journalism in 2008, then spent eight years doing a wide variety of things, including a stint as a commercial beekeeper. I went back to school in 2016, earning my PhD in biology at the University of Utah, where I built models to understand the role of information and learning in foraging animals. I then held postdocs at the University of Pennsylvania (Penn) and Princeton, where I extended those models and applied them to how *people* learn from each other and make decisions (details on [CV](/cv/) and [Research](/research/) pages).

Raised partly on Pacific islands and partly in the deserts of the Mountain West, I'm at my best when I'm outside — mountaineering, sea kayaking, trekking, paragliding, or roasting my own coffee beans!
